# Starter mission curriculum

The version 1 curriculum contains nine authored missions: three each in Spanish, French, and Dutch. Each language offers café orders, informal introductions, and arranging an appointment. The task is deliberately narrow: complete an everyday exchange using four useful phrase chunks, rather than attain a claimed proficiency level.

## Content and assessment design

Each mission contains four chunks with English meanings, a concise explanation, and selected acceptable alternatives where appropriate. Alternatives are examples, not an exhaustive list of valid language. A short bilingual dialogue supplies two comprehension questions. A prompted speaking task has a model response and a three-part self-assessment checklist. Model responses show one possible answer; speaking is not independently graded for pronunciation, fluency, or grammar.

The transfer material changes facts that matter to the task. Café customers reverse a preference and stay rather than take away; introductions distinguish a new person's origin from their current home; appointment dialogues reject both an initial day and a proposed time before confirming a different time. Transfer prompts do not supply target-language hints. Production tasks reuse taught chunks and contrasts from the phrase explanations, with new appointment details encountered in the transfer dialogue.

Transfer dialogues are initially unseen within the mission. The starter bank has one transfer variation per mission, so a repeat of that variation must not be described as a fresh unseen assessment. More independently authored variants and delayed checks would be needed for a robust evaluation of generalization.

The dialogues share task structure across languages while using language-specific phrases. Social conversations use an informal peer register; service interactions use polite requests. Spanish examples use broadly understandable phrasing; French and Dutch reflect common European usage. Local preferences, formality norms, and additional natural alternatives may differ. Prices are fictional story details, not current prices.

## References consulted

The original dialogues are authored for this project, not copied from the resources below. These references support specific usage decisions; they do not certify the complete curriculum.

- [University of Texas at Austin, Tex's French Grammar: conditional](https://laits.utexas.edu/tex/gr/tac1.html) explains polite uses of the conditional, including *je voudrais*. This supports the request pattern used in the French café and appointment missions.
- [University of Texas at Austin, Français interactif](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/fi/) provides a university-produced beginner French curriculum with everyday interactions, used as a scope reference.
- [SpanishDictionary: polite restaurant phrases](https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/polite-restaurant-phrases-in-spanish) supplies reference examples of polite requests and restaurant vocabulary. The mission uses its own dialogue and explanations.
- [Taaladvies.net: dank je wel / dankjewel](https://taaladvies.net/dank-je-wel-dankjewel/) explicitly accepts both *dank u wel* and *dankuwel* as expressions of thanks. Both are accepted in the Dutch appointment phrase.
- [DutchGrammar: examples of open questions](https://www.dutchgrammar.com/en/?n=WordOrder.52) explains interrogative word order. This informed the Dutch question explanations. The page was available through search retrieval; a separate direct request for its adjacent general question page returned HTTP 403.

References checked on 5 September 2026. These language references are not evidence that this app accelerates learning by a measured amount.

## Review status and remaining work

The material was authored and checked by an AI coding agent for grammatical plausibility, dialogue/translation alignment, unambiguous answer choices, and consistency of the changed transfer facts. Automated tests validate identifiers, question indices, phrase references, distinct choices, complete tasks, and selected semantic contrasts. Passing those tests does not establish linguistic correctness.

**No qualified teacher or native-speaker review has been obtained. No learning-effectiveness study has been performed.** The app must not describe this material as expert-reviewed, certified, or proven to accelerate learning. A qualified reviewer for each language should assess naturalness, register, regional alternatives, translations, and task difficulty before those claims are considered. Audio uses the app's available speech system; there are no commissioned human recordings in this bank.

Content changes that alter the learning target or accepted meaning should increment `Mission.version`. Mission IDs include language, and phrase IDs are local to their mission, so stored evidence cannot mix the same task across languages.
